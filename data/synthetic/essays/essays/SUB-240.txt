Essay 2: Should we let machines decide?
Gauri Shinde (SUB-240)

Automated decisions promise speed and consistency, and they often deliver both. The cost of an automated mistake is rarely paid by the people who chose to automate.

Consistency is only a virtue when the rule being applied consistently is a fair one. When a decision goes wrong, a person can explain and apologise; a system can only be audited.

We should automate the routine and keep a named person responsible for the rest.
