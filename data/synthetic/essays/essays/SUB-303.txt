Essay 2: Should we let machines decide?
Pallavi Rao (SUB-303)

Every week another task moves from a person to an algorithm. Consistency is only a virtue when the rule being applied consistently is a fair one.

Automation does not remove judgment from work; it moves judgment to the people who design the system and hides it from the people who live with its results. The cost of an automated mistake is rarely paid by the people who chose to automate. When a decision goes wrong, a person can explain and apologise; a system can only be audited.

Machines should advise, and people should decide when the stakes are high.
