Essay 2: Should we let machines decide?
Manav Chopra (SUB-221)

Every week another task moves from a person to an algorithm. When a decision goes wrong, a person can explain and apologise; a system can only be audited.

As Okafor puts it, "Automation does not remove judgment from work; it moves judgment to the people who design the system and hides it from the people who live with its results." (Okafor, 2024, p. 41). Consistency is only a virtue when the rule being applied consistently is a fair one. The cost of an automated mistake is rarely paid by the people who chose to automate.

Machines should advise, and people should decide when the stakes are high.

References
Okafor, A. (2024). Machines and Meaning. Lagos: Harbour Press, p. 41.
