Essay 2: Should we let machines decide?
Harsh Vora (SUB-237)

The question is no longer whether machines can decide, but whether they should. The cost of an automated mistake is rarely paid by the people who chose to automate.

Some decisions are reversible and cheap, and those are the ones machines handle best. A hospital that triages patients by software can see more people, but it can also miss the patient who does not fit the pattern.

The right test is whether someone affected by the decision could still appeal to a human.
